# Foreign assets, foreign income, residency (Schedules FA / FSI / TR)

## Residency decides scope — compute it, don't assume

Section 6 day-count: 182 days in India, or the 60+365 combination (with NRI/PIO variants and the
deemed-residency rule for high-income citizens — verify thresholds for the AY). Outcomes:
- **ROR**: worldwide income taxable; Schedule FA mandatory for any foreign asset.
- **RNOR / NR**: foreign income generally outside scope; Schedule FA not required; ITR-1/4 barred
  for NR.
Ask for day counts (this FY + preceding years) only when a foreign signal exists: passport
stamps mentioned, LRS rows, employer RSUs, overseas payroll.

## Schedule FA — holding is reportable, selling is irrelevant

Every foreign asset held at ANY time in the calendar year (note: FA uses calendar year, not FY —
verify the current form's convention): foreign brokerage/bank accounts, employer RSUs/ESPPs in a
foreign parent (vested = held, even if never sold), foreign property, crypto on foreign exchanges
per current guidance. Non-disclosure sits under the Black Money Act with penalties wildly out of
proportion to the asset — treat FA completeness as a red line.

RSU mechanics: vesting value = salary perquisite (already in 12BA); the shares then sit in FA;
dividends = foreign income (Schedule FSI); sale = capital gains with acquisition cost = vesting
FMV. Currency conversion per the prescribed telegraphic-transfer rates.

## Double-tax relief

Foreign tax withheld (US dividend withholding, foreign payroll tax) → treaty relief via Schedule TR
with **Form 67 filed before the return**. Late Form 67 = credit denied in processing. DTAA rates
override domestic where beneficial; cite the article in the data pack.

## What is NOT foreign for these purposes

Forex-card spending abroad, LRS remittances for travel/education (those are TCS credits, not
assets), and INR payouts from foreign platforms to a resident (ordinary Indian business income).
A genuine account opened abroad to receive such payouts, however, IS an FA row.
